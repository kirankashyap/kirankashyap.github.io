From the Prashna Upanishad

Six questions for Pippalada

Six students who had studied the Vedas came to the sage Pippalada, each with a bundle of firewood and a question. He made them wait a year before they could ask. These are the six questions and his answers, told simply.

Six studentsKabandhi, Bhargava, Kausalya, Gargya, Satyakama, Sukesha
Pippaladaa forest sage who makes his students wait
Six students with firewood

The conversation follows the Prashna Upanishad closely, and the small verse numbers show where each idea comes from. All six questions are in the Upanishad; the students’ short follow-up questions are added here to carry the telling. This is a simple retelling, some verses are shortened or left out, and the explanations follow Adi Shankaracharya’s commentary.

Pippalada’s name means “one who eats pippala figs”, the fruit of the sacred fig tree, so he is drawn under one, with its heart-shaped leaves and small figs above him. Like other forest sages in the tradition, he has a white beard and his hair tied up in a knot, with ochre cloth, rudraksha beads and a water pot.

For the full text with commentary, see Eight Upanishads, Volume 2, translated by Swami Gambhirananda (Advaita Ashrama, Ramakrishna Order).

Also told this way: Shaunaka asks Angiras, from the Mundaka Upanishad, which belongs to the Atharva Veda like this one.
