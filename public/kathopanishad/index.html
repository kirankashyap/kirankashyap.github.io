From the Katha Upanishad

Nachiketa talks with Yama

A boy goes to the house of Death and asks the biggest question of all. This is their conversation, told simply.

Nachiketaa brave, curious boy
Yamalord of death, and a kind teacher
The gift

The conversation follows the Katha Upanishad closely. Small verse numbers show where each idea comes from, so older readers can look them up. The wording is a simple retelling, not a translation, and the explanations follow Adi Shankaracharya’s commentary.

Yama is drawn as tradition describes him: dark as a rain cloud, in red garments, crowned, holding the staff and the noose, with his buffalo resting nearby.
