From the Kena Upanishad

By whom?

A student asks his teacher what moves the mind, the breath and the senses. The answer points to something none of them can reach. Then the teacher tells a story about the gods, a blade of grass and a wonder none of them could name. This is the Kena Upanishad, told simply.

The studentwho wants to know what moves the mind
The teachera forest teacher the Upanishad does not name
By whom?

The conversation follows the Kena Upanishad closely, and the small verse numbers show where each idea comes from. The student’s short follow-up questions are added here to carry the telling. This is a simple retelling, some verses are shortened, and the explanations follow Adi Shankaracharya’s commentary.

The Upanishad names neither the teacher nor the student. The teacher is drawn under a banyan tree with ochre cloth, rudraksha beads and a water pot, like other forest sages in the tradition. Brahman appears to the gods as a yaksha they cannot recognise, so it is drawn only as a light.
