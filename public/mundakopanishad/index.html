From the Mundaka Upanishad

Shaunaka asks Angiras

A learned householder who had everything came to a forest sage with a single question: what is the one thing that, once known, makes everything known? This is their conversation, told simply.

Shaunakaa learned, wealthy householder
Angirasa forest sage, and a patient teacher
The question

The conversation follows the Mundaka Upanishad closely. Small verse numbers show where each idea comes from, so older readers can look them up. In the Upanishad Shaunaka asks only one question; his other questions are added here to carry the telling. The wording is a simple retelling, not a translation, and the explanations follow Adi Shankaracharya’s commentary.

Angiras is drawn the way tradition pictures a forest sage: white-bearded, hair tied up in a knot, in ochre cloth with a string of rudraksha beads, seated on a deerskin under a tree with his water pot beside him. The name Mundaka is often explained as “the shaven head,” a sign that the text was meant for those who had renounced the world.

For the full text with commentary, see Eight Upanishads, Volume 2, translated by Swami Gambhirananda (Advaita Ashrama, Ramakrishna Order).
